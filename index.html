About Me

Research

Climate Shocks and Sustainable Investment Behavior: Evidence from Alipay's Green Fund Platform

Job Market Paper, Single Author

Abstract: This study examines how personal experiences with abnormally hot temperatures influence retail investors' green fund investments in China. Using a unique dataset from Alipay, which tracks fund holdings and eco-friendly behaviors, I find that exposure to temperature shocks leads to a 2.15% increase in the number of green fund purchases and an 18.43% rise in green investment volume. I also investigate the impact on the percentage of green fund purchases and the proportion of green investment relative to total investments, finding that, compared to the sample mean, they increase by 35.92% and 42.85%, respectively, following the shock. These effects remain significant even after accounting for financial returns, suggesting that social motives play a key role. Additionally, I find that exposure to temperature shocks leads to increased engagement in eco-friendly activities, indicating that personal experiences with climate extremes prompt socially responsible retail investors to align their financial decisions with their environmental values. The impact is more pronounced for males and lower-income investors, who typically exhibit lower environmental concerns. The impact on green investment is long term. There is no strong continuation or reversal in green investment behaviors in the 2 to 6 months after the shock. The results suggest that investors quickly adjust their beliefs about climate risks. These findings highlight the role of climate experiences in shaping sustainable investment behavior.

Bridging the Gender Gap in Credit Access: The Role of Bank Competition

with Pulak Ghosh, Chen Lin, Jian Zhang

R&R at Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis

Abstract: We examine the impact of increased bank competition on credit access for female-led small businesses. Exploiting the 2005 branching reform in India and a regression discontinuity design, we find that increased bank competition significantly improves credit access for female-led firms, with the probability of borrowing increasing by 6% and loan amounts by 27%. However, such effects are absent for male-led firms. Consistent with Becker's (1957) theory of discrimination, the effect is more pronounced in areas with discriminatory norms and branches with a higher proportion of male staff. The reform also boosted female entrepreneurship, indicating an improvement in women's economic status.

Seeing is Believing: Evidence from a Technology-enabled, Bottom-up Approach to Sustainable Development and Environmental Solutions

with Jiafu An, Difang Huang, and Chen Lin

Abstract: This study assesses public concerns about local environmental issues and evaluates the effectiveness of Ant Forest, the world's largest bottom-up program with over 550 million users, in translating awareness into green behaviors. Our findings reveal that individuals are highly responsive to local air pollution events, significantly increasing their efforts to combat air pollution when its adverse effects become apparent. The study provides insights for future programs aimed at involving the general population in addressing environmental issues.
